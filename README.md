## This is our shared workbench.

**KITTY × TWEAK LABS (KTL)** brings together KrazyKitty’s interconnected Development Universe and King Tweak’s Crimson Workshop.

We like understanding how things work, building environments that feel like our own, and asking what happens when we change the architecture. Some ideas start in a notebook. Others start in a terminal. They earn their place in our research through experiments, measurements and documentation.

This repository is our **research and coordination home**: shared plans, architecture proposals, research standards and future experiment records. Individual implementations belong in their own project repositories.

**Our aim:** build systems we understand, test the claims we make, and leave enough evidence for someone else to follow the work.

## 💜❤️ Where our worlds connect

We both work across AI, systems and software. Our different perspectives give us more ways to question an idea, design an experiment and recognise a limitation.

Kitty’s AMD/ROCm environment and Tweak’s NVIDIA/CUDA environment also give us a practical reason to investigate portability and hardware behaviour. Shared infrastructure is a research direction we develop through explicit milestones.

**KittyLM remains KittyLM. TweakTron remains TweakTron.** Each retains its own identity and ownership. A future joint model such as **KittyTron** is a proposed research outcome, not a replacement for either independent project.

## Roadmap // ambition in manageable steps

1. **Foundations:** document ownership, scope, research standards and milestone acceptance criteria.
2. **Baselines:** establish repeatable tokenizer, model and hardware measurements.
3. **Shared tooling:** develop dataset, evaluation and experiment tools around demonstrated needs.
4. **Infrastructure:** add storage, orchestration, monitoring and compute capacity as workloads justify them.
5. **Architecture experiments:** investigate memory, recurrence, sparse computation and long-context behaviour.
6. **Joint models:** use validated findings to decide whether and how to build KittyTron.

This is a proposed sequence, not a claim that these phases have been completed.

## Research records // leave a trail someone can follow

An experiment should connect its question and method to its code revision, configuration, dataset and tokenizer versions, random seeds, hardware, software environment, outputs and limitations.

| Record prefix | Intended use |
|---|---|
| `KTL-RFC` | Research proposals |
| `KTL-ADR` | Architecture decisions |
| `KTL-EXP` | Experiment records |
| `KTL-BENCH` | Benchmarks |
| `KTL-REPORT` | Engineering reports |
| `KTL-PAPER` | Research papers |

We distinguish **Verified** (direct evidence), **Reported** (attributed results), **Inferred** (reasoned interpretation), **Proposed** (future work) and **Unknown** (insufficient evidence).

A published model or dataset remains attributed to its original author. Collection membership and research references do not transfer ownership. Release status, research maturity and evidence are separate questions.

## Workshop principles

- **Build before scale.** Start with an experiment small enough to understand.
- **Measure before claiming.** Keep results connected to methods and limitations.
- **Understand before abstracting.** Learn what the system is doing underneath.
- **Reproduce before optimising.** Establish a baseline before chasing speed.
- **Secure by design.** Make permissions, isolation and defensive engineering part of the work.
- **Let the workload justify the hardware.** Every expansion should enable a useful experiment.
- **Keep the personality.** Creative interfaces and rigorous engineering belong together.
- **Protect independence.** Shared work has explicit scope, credit and agreement from both builders.
